Make and prove a conjecture about a common divisor shared by all integers of the form
$7^n - 4$ for $n$ a non-negative integer.

\begin{proposition}
    All integers of the form $7^n - 4$ for a non-negative integer $n$ are divisible by $3$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof} The proof is by induction on $n$. Consider a base case of $n=0$. Observe,

    \[
        7^0 - 4 = 1 - 4 = -3 
    \]

    $-3$ is divisible by $3$, so the result holds. Now suppose that the result holds for some 
    non-negative integer $k$. That is, $7^k - 4$ can be expressed as the product of $3$ and 
    some other integer $j$. Observe how we can rearrange this identity:

    \[\begin{split}
        7^k - 4 &= 3j \\
        7^k &= 3j + 4
    \end{split}\]

    Now consider the case for $k+1$. Observe,

    \[\begin{aligned}
         &\ 7^{k+1} - 4 \\
        =&\ 7(7^k) - 4 \\
        =&\ 7(3j + 4) - 4 & [\text{by the inductive hypothesis}] \\
        =&\ 21j + 28 - 4 \\
        =&\ 21j + 24 \\
        =&\ 3(7j + 8)
    \end{aligned}
    \]

    $7j + 8$ is itself an integer. Thus, the result holds for the inductive case. Therefore,
    by the principle of mathematical induction, the result holds for any integer $k \ge 0$.

\end{proof}
